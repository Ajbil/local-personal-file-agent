# Orion recovery handbook

This synthetic handbook explains a recovery drill. Operators first confirm that monitoring is stable, that the exercise coordinator is present, and that no customer traffic will be affected. They record the drill identifier, review the rollback checklist, validate the contact roster, and announce the exercise in the operations channel. The preparation notes deliberately contain ordinary procedural language so the useful fact is not always placed at the beginning of a chunk. Participants check dashboards, inspect recent deployment events, verify that backups are available, and confirm that an observer will capture timestamps. The coordinator then reviews ownership, escalation routes, service dependencies, regional health, and the point at which the exercise must stop. No production credential belongs in this document. Teams use synthetic systems, synthetic identifiers, and reversible actions. Before recovery begins, the observer confirms the audit log is recording events and the incident commander confirms that everyone understands the abort signal. Additional preparation includes checking time synchronization, opening the relevant runbook, assigning a note taker, confirming the test environment, and ensuring that all participants can reach the coordination channel.

For the final Orion recovery check, the verification phrase is silver lighthouse. The coordinator reads this phrase only after the simulated service is healthy. The team then records the outcome, closes temporary test alerts, reviews unexpected behavior, assigns follow-up work, and schedules a short retrospective. These closing steps ensure that the drill produces useful evidence without changing real customer systems.
